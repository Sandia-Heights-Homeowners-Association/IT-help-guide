## Committee Folder Organization

Every committee has both an Office folder and a SharePoint folder/site. Those
folders provide a consistent structure so staff, volunteers, committee members,
and Board members know where to find information.

To make that system work, committee-related materials need to stay in those
locations. Office-only working documents, separate issues, staff-only drafts, or
other material that is not part of the committee record should have its own
workspace outside the committee folders.

Committee document libraries are owned by the committee's Microsoft 365 group.
Office staff administer the group-connected SharePoint site and membership.
They are not folders owned by Anna or another individual user.

External committee members use the committee SharePoint site in their browser.
They should never use OneDrive for committee documents.

Internal office users may add the SharePoint **Documents** library to OneDrive
for convenience:

1. Open the committee SharePoint site while signed in to the office account.
2. Open **Documents**.
3. Choose **Add shortcut to OneDrive** or **Add shortcut to My files**.
4. Confirm the library appears under **My files** for that office account.

Each internal user must add the shortcut while signed in to their own account.

## Sharing Files With A Board Or Committee Group

Do not use **Share** or **Copy link** to give a Board or committee group access
to a file. Move the file into the folder that belongs to the intended group.
Access comes from membership in that group's SharePoint/Microsoft 365 group.

| Who needs the file? | Put it here | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Office staff only | **Board - Office** or the matching committee Office folder | Work from the internal office folder |
| Board members | **Board - SharePoint** | Board members open it from the [Board SharePoint site](https://shha.sharepoint.com/sites/BOARD) |
| Committee members | The matching committee **- SharePoint** folder | Members open it from their committee SharePoint site |

The **Board - SharePoint** folder forms the Board SharePoint document library.
The **Board - Office** folder is staff-only working space.

This distinction matters for Board and committee members using personal email
addresses such as Gmail. **Share** and **Copy link** work reliably only for
`@sandiahomeowners.org` users. External members should access files through the
appropriate SharePoint site. A link points to a file; it does not grant the
group access.

## SharePoint File Access

Each committee SharePoint site is managed through a SharePoint/Microsoft 365
group. Committee group members have write access to that committee's document
library, whether they use an SHHA account or an approved external account.

Committee members can create, upload, edit, move, and delete files within their
committee SharePoint site. Use the SharePoint **Documents** library for the
official committee files, and use **Move to** instead of downloading and
re-uploading when reorganizing files.

Each committee SharePoint site also has a **Documents Shared with Board** folder.
These folders are linked from the [Board SharePoint site](https://shha.sharepoint.com/sites/BOARD)
so Board members can find committee reference materials in one place. Use this
folder for enduring documents that Board members may need months or years later,
such as legal analyses, committee procedures, active dispute/project background,
committee charters, resident welcome materials, and other non-public reference
documents. Committee chairs should add useful files; office staff can assist.

Office staff can update committee access by opening the committee SharePoint
site, choosing **Site permissions** from **Settings**, and adding or removing
members from the committee group. Membership changes automatically update access
to the committee's SharePoint documents.

## Microsoft Forms For RSVPs And Surveys

For a headcount, RSVP, quick poll, or resident/committee survey, the usual
answer is: contact the office and ask them to create the form.

That keeps ownership with SHHA instead of one volunteer's personal Forms
account, and it gives office staff a predictable place to find the response link
and results later. Tiny convenience now beats a future scavenger hunt through
old email.

Licensed users with an `@sandiahomeowners.org` account can also create a form:

1. Go to [forms.microsoft.com](https://forms.microsoft.com), or sign in at
   [office.com](https://office.com), open the app launcher, and choose
   **Forms**.
2. Choose **New Form**.
3. Add a clear title, description, and questions.
4. Use **Preview** and submit one test response.
5. Choose **Collect responses**.
6. Choose who can respond, then copy the response link.

For anything that includes residents, committee members using personal email
addresses, or other external users, use a public response link such as
**Anyone can respond**. If you need names or email addresses, ask for them as
questions on the form. Microsoft's automatic "record name" and "one response
per person" options only work well when everyone signs in with a supported
Microsoft work or school account.

Forms are not shared like Word or PDF files. A form will not appear in someone
else's OneDrive or SharePoint library just because you shared it. People reach
the form by using the link, so put the link somewhere easy to find: the email
announcement, calendar item, committee SharePoint note, or wiki page.

Do not confuse the two link types:

- **Collect responses** is the link respondents use to fill out the form.
- **Collaborate** is an admin/editor link. Only send it to people who should
  edit the form or view the response data.

For SHHA records, export important results to Excel and save the file in the
appropriate SharePoint document library.

## Temporary Task Group Pattern

For a temporary task force, the usual setup is:

1. A Microsoft 365 Group mailing list for all members.
2. No shared mailbox for the task-force lead or committee chair.
3. Optional friendly alias, such as `wildfire@sandiahomeowners.org`.
4. `admin@sandiahomeowners.org` included in the group for archive continuity.
5. Test email delivery before announcing the address.
